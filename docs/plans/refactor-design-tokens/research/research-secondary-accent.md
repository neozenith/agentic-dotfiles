# What a Secondary Accent Actually Does — evidence from shipped design systems

Brief for the **[OPEN]** in [`CURATION.md`](../CURATION.md) ("What is the secondary accent *for*?"), which currently lists `accent.secondary` as a **required** seed field. Verbatim quotes, every claim sourced; `[UNVERIFIED]` marks anything not confirmed from a primary source. Fetched 2026-09-03.

**Verdict: the seed field is not load-bearing, and "required" is wrong.** Nine of ten systems surveyed have exactly **one** accent hue. "Secondary" almost everywhere names a *prominence rung* rendered in **neutral grey**, not a second brand colour. The one system shipping three real hues (daisyUI) calls two "optional". The one system that derives a second hue (M3) derives it from a single seed by arithmetic — the designer still supplies one colour.

## 1. Material Design 3 — the math confirmed, and it is not what the framing implies

**The brief's claim is confirmed exactly**, but not from `SchemeTonalSpot` — upstream refactored, and the palettes now live in `DynamicScheme`'s spec methods ([`dynamic_scheme.ts`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/material-foundation/material-color-utilities/main/typescript/dynamiccolor/dynamic_scheme.ts), `main`, lines 653/684/721):

```ts
getPrimaryPalette:   case Variant.TONAL_SPOT: return TonalPalette.fromHueAndChroma(sourceColorHct.hue, 36.0);
getSecondaryPalette: case Variant.TONAL_SPOT: return TonalPalette.fromHueAndChroma(sourceColorHct.hue, 16.0);
getTertiaryPalette:  case Variant.RAINBOW: case Variant.TONAL_SPOT:
  return TonalPalette.fromHueAndChroma(math.sanitizeDegreesDouble(sourceColorHct.hue + 60.0), 24.0);
```

⚠️ Two citation traps. Do **not** cite the legacy `CorePalette` — it uses `primary = max(48, chroma)`, not 36 ([`core_palette.dart`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/material-foundation/material-color-utilities/main/dart/lib/palettes/core_palette.dart)). And **the 36 is spec-versioned**: those are `SPEC_2021` values (still the library default); under `SPEC_2025`/`2026` primary drops to 32 (26 on phone+dark) and tertiary becomes a rotation array — but **secondary stays `(h, 16)` in every spec version**, which is itself the finding. `error` is hard-coded at `fromHueAndChroma(25.0, 84.0)` (line 268): even M3 refuses to derive semantics from the seed.

**Is secondary ever a different hue? Yes — in exactly 2 of 9 variants**, from those same three methods:

| Variant | primary | secondary | secondary hue |
|---|---|---|---|
| `TONAL_SPOT` (default) | `(h, 36)` | `(h, 16)` | **same**, lower chroma |
| `CONTENT` / `FIDELITY` | `(h, c)` source | `(h, max(c−32, c×0.5))` | **same**, lower chroma |
| `RAINBOW` / `NEUTRAL` / `MONOCHROME` | `(h, 48)` / `(h, 12)` / `(h, 0)` | `(h, 16)` / `(h, 8)` / `(h, 0)` | **same** |
| `FRUIT_SALAD` | `(h−50, 48)` | `(h−50, 36)` | same (rotated) |
| `EXPRESSIVE` | `(h+240, 40)` | `getRotatedHue(…[45,95,45,20,45,90,45,45,45])`, 24 | **different** |
| `VIBRANT` | `(h, 200)` | `getRotatedHue(…[18,15,10,12,15,18,15,12,12])`, 24 | **different** |

"Same hue, lower chroma" is the rule (7/9); a rotated hue is the expressive exception (2/9) — via piecewise rotation tables, never a second author-supplied colour.

**Usage guidance**, Google's own words ([developer.android.com](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/designsystems/material3)): *"Primary is the base color, used for main components like prominent buttons, active states, and the tint of elevated surfaces."* / *"The secondary key color is used for **less prominent components in the UI, such as filter chips**, and expands the opportunity for color expression."* / *"The tertiary key color is used to derive the roles of **contrasting accents that can be used to balance primary and secondary colors or bring enhanced attention to an element**."*

⚠️ **The most-quoted M3 sentence about secondary is stale M2 text — do not cite it as guidance.** Compose's [`ColorScheme.kt`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/androidx/androidx/androidx-main/compose/material3/material3/src/commonMain/kotlin/androidx/compose/material3/ColorScheme.kt) (lines 58–70) says: *"The secondary color provides more ways to accent and distinguish your product. Secondary colors are best for: - Floating action buttons - Selection controls, like checkboxes and radio buttons - Highlighting selected text - Links and headlines"*. That survived unmigrated from M2, where `secondary` *was* the accent, and M3's own tokens contradict it — `CheckboxTokens`/`RadioButtonTokens` use `Primary` for the checked state, and m3.material.io puts FABs on primary. `secondaryContainer`'s KDoc is contentless ("A tonal color to be used in containers").

**So the real definition is where components consume the token, and it is unambiguous: `secondaryContainer` is the *selected / active state* surface.** `NavigationBarTokens.ItemActiveIndicatorColor` and `FilterChipTokens.ElevatedSelectedContainerColor` are both `= ColorSchemeKeyTokens.SecondaryContainer` with `OnSecondaryContainer` for content ([NavigationBarTokens.kt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/androidx/androidx/androidx-main/compose/material3/material3/src/commonMain/kotlin/androidx/compose/material3/tokens/NavigationBarTokens.kt), [FilterChipTokens.kt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/androidx/androidx/androidx-main/compose/material3/material3/src/commonMain/kotlin/androidx/compose/material3/tokens/FilterChipTokens.kt)), as is `FilledTonalButtonTokens.ContainerColor`. Corroborated in two other implementations: MDC-Android (`m3_comp_nav_bar_item_active_indicator_color` → `?attr/colorSecondaryContainer`; `m3_chip_background_color.xml` `state_selected` → same) and material-web (`_md-comp-navigation-bar.scss` `'active-indicator-color'` → `secondary-container`).

**Bare `secondary` is far narrower than the container** — its clearest use is the toggle-button *selected fill* (`TonalButtonTokens`: `SelectedContainerColor = Secondary` against `UnselectedContainerColor = SecondaryContainer`, mirrored in `FilledTonalIconButtonTokens`). How narrow: material-web's baseline token set (all 95 files in `tokens/versions/v0_192/`) has **zero** references to bare `secondary`. **Job of the pair: "selected, but quieter than primary."**

**Required? You never choose it.** `ColorScheme.fromSeed` takes one colour; secondary, tertiary and both neutrals are imputed — Flutter: *"Using the `seedColor` as a starting point, a set of tonal palettes are constructed."* ([api.flutter.dev](https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/material/ColorScheme/ColorScheme.fromSeed.html)). **The system most cited as proving "you need three colours" asks the designer for one.**

## 2. Bootstrap — the source of the convention, and its secondary is grey

Confirmed from [`scss/_variables.scss`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/twbs/bootstrap/main/scss/_variables.scss) (`main`, lines 301–308 against 10/15/18/39): `$primary: $blue` (`#0d6efd`), **`$secondary: $gray-600` (`#6c757d`)**, `$success: $green`, `$info: $cyan`, `$warning: $yellow`, `$danger: $red`, `$light: $gray-100`, `$dark: $gray-900`. **Claim confirmed: a grey, not a second brand hue.**

Bootstrap's own meaning table says it is *de-emphasis*, not identity ([customize/color](https://getbootstrap.com/docs/5.3/customize/color/)): *"**Primary —** Main theme color, used for hyperlinks, focus styles, and component and form active states."* / *"**Secondary —** Use the `color` option for **lighter text**. Use the `bg` option for dividers and to indicate **disabled** component states."* / *"**Tertiary —** Use the `color` option for **even lighter text**."*

The clincher — in 5.3 the secondary *role* literally absorbed `muted` ([utilities/colors](https://getbootstrap.com/docs/5.3/utilities/colors/)): *"the `.text-muted` utility has been deprecated as of v5.3.0. Its default value has also been **reassigned to the new `--bs-secondary-color` CSS variable**."* The buttons page defines no per-variant meaning at all, only *"Bootstrap includes several button variants, each serving its own semantic purpose"* ([components/buttons](https://getbootstrap.com/docs/5.3/components/buttons/)). **`.btn-secondary` — the most-copied source of this convention — never meant "your second brand colour". It meant grey.**

## 3. Tailwind — no semantic layer at all; the framing is downstream

**Core Tailwind v4 ships no `primary`/`secondary`/`accent` whatsoever.** Grepping the shipped [`theme.css`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tailwindlabs/tailwindcss/main/packages/tailwindcss/theme.css) (`main`) for `primary|secondary|accent|brand` returns **zero matches**; `--color-*` holds named hues only (`slate`…`rose`, plus newer `mauve`, `mist`, `olive`, `taupe`). [Docs](https://tailwindcss.com/docs/theme) call `--color-*` merely *"Color utilities like `bg-red-500`, `text-sky-300`, and many more"*, and [docs/colors](https://tailwindcss.com/docs/colors) gives **no** brand/accent guidance — "primary"/"secondary"/"brand" don't appear in its prose. Customisation is mechanical: *"Use `@theme` to add custom colors to your project under the `--color-*` theme namespace."* **The primary/secondary framing is purely downstream.**

| Downstream | `secondary` value | Second brand hue? |
|---|---|---|
| **shadcn/ui** | `oklch(0.97 0 0)` light / `oklch(0.269 0 0)` dark — **chroma 0** | **No** — every default token but `--destructive` has chroma exactly 0 |
| **Preline** | `var(--color-stone-900)` near-black | **No** — "Secondary emphasis" |
| **Flowbite** | none (uses `--color-fg-brand*`) | N/A |
| **daisyUI** | `oklch(65% 0.241 354)` magenta | **Yes** — the sole genuine three-hue system |

shadcn's [theming docs](https://ui.shadcn.com/docs/theming) describe an *emphasis ladder*, not a hue pairing: `primary` = "High-emphasis actions and brand surfaces", `secondary` = "**Lower-emphasis** filled actions", and critically `accent` = "**Interactive hover, focus, and active surfaces**" — an interaction-state surface, not a second brand colour. daisyUI is the honest outlier and marks its extras optional ([daisyui.com/docs/colors](https://daisyui.com/docs/colors/)): secondary is *"The **optional**, secondary color of your brand"*, accent *"The **optional**, accent color of your brand"*. Only `primary` is unqualified.

## 4. Carbon, Primer, Atlassian — three confirmed absences, three different reasons

**IBM Carbon — no secondary accent.** One brand hue, stated: *"The core blue family serves as the primary action color across all IBM products and experiences. **Additional colors are used sparingly and purposefully.**"* ([color/overview](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/color/overview/)). "Secondary" is button *hierarchy*, and it is grey: `--cds-button-primary:#0f62fe` vs `--cds-button-secondary:#393939` (Gray 80) ([color/tokens](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/color/tokens/)). Carbon says why: *"Although secondary buttons have less visual prominence because they are less saturated than their primary counterparts, they are still tonally heavy."* ([button/usage](https://carbondesignsystem.com/components/button/usage/)). ⚠️ Trap: Carbon's only `accent`-named tokens are `$layer-accent-01/02/03` = `#e0e0e0` — a **neutral surface**, not an accent hue.

**GitHub Primer — singular `fgColor-accent`, confirmed.** Exactly one accent in the functional set: `accent: { $value: '{base.color.blue.5}', $description: 'Accent text for links and interactive elements' }`, with `link` an *alias* of it ([fgColor.json5](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/primer/primitives/main/src/tokens/functional/color/fgColor.json5)). Roles are emphasis levels of that one hue (`fgColor-accent`, `bgColor-accent-emphasis|muted`, `borderColor-accent-emphasis|muted`). Grepping [`button.json5`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/primer/primitives/main/src/tokens/component/button.json5) for "secondary" returns **zero hits** — the variant is `default`, on a neutral control surface. Strongest data point: **Primer's primary button is not the accent** — `primary: { bgColor: { rest: '{bgColor.success.emphasis}' } }`, i.e. green; accent blue is reserved for *"Links, selected, active, and focus states"* ([foundations/color](https://primer.style/foundations/color/overview)).

**Atlassian — `accent` is a categorical palette that means nothing, by design** ([foundations/color/accents](https://atlassian.design/foundations/color/accents)): *"Accent colors help differentiate interface elements such as sticky notes, color pickers, and project icons."* / *"**Accents don't communicate any specific meaning to users**, unlike success, warning, or other color roles."* / *"consider whether **the color could be swapped for another without changing the experience**"* / *"**Avoid mixing different accent colors**"*. All 10 accent hues' shipped token descriptions carry the phrase *"when there is no meaning tied to the color"* (`@atlaskit/tokens` metadata). Brand is separate and singular — one hue (`--ds-background-brand-bold: #1868db`) laddered `subtlest → bold → boldest`. Across all 582 shipped tokens, **zero** contain "secondary"; the button appearance is `default` (neutral), not `secondary` (`@atlaskit/button` `types.d.ts`).

## 5. Polaris, Spectrum, Radix — the emphasis ladder is neutral, and two say so explicitly

**Shopify Polaris — "secondary" is a slot in the token grammar, not a hue.** The structure is `--p-color-[element]-[role?]-[prominence?]-[state?]` ([polaris-tokens-structure.md](https://github.com/Shopify/polaris/blob/main/polaris-tokens/polaris-tokens-structure.md)): `brand` is a **role**, `secondary`/`tertiary` are **prominence**, orthogonal — proven by `color-bg-fill-info-secondary` (*"communicating important information … with a secondary level of prominence"*), where prominence stacks *inside* a role. Bare `-secondary` resolves to greys (`color-bg-fill-secondary: colors.gray[6]`). And Polaris gives the explicit prohibition, as a **Don't** ([design/colors](https://polaris.shopify.com/design/colors)): *"**Use multiple brand roles in the same area, this can confuse merchants as to which action is the most appropriate in the current context.**"*

**Adobe Spectrum — one accent; its "primary"/"secondary" buttons are literally grey** ([page/button](https://spectrum.adobe.com/page/button/)): the **accent** button *"communicates strong emphasis … **Don't use more than 3 accent buttons in the same view**"* and renders `blue-500`; **primary** is *"for **medium emphasis**"* and renders **`gray-800`**; **secondary** is *"for **low emphasis**"* and renders **`gray-300`**. Accent is one of five semantic meanings — *"Spectrum's semantic meanings include informative, accent, negative, notice, and positive"* — with blue serving both informative and accent ([color-system](https://spectrum.adobe.com/page/color-system/)). Re-theming *swaps* the one accent (blue → indigo for Express, [theming](https://spectrum.adobe.com/page/theming/)); it never adds a second. Spectrum also warns against adjacent competing hues: *"**Avoid placing colors directly on top of or adjacent to other colors that have a high degree of hue contrast with near-equal saturation or lightness.** These scenarios create a visual illusion of depth or vibration (chromostereopsis)"* ([using-color](https://spectrum.adobe.com/page/using-color/)).

**Radix Themes — one `accentColor` + one `grayColor`, confirmed by exhaustive search.** *"Accent color is the most dominant color in your theme. It is used for primary buttons, links and other interactive elements."* … *"Your accent color will be **automatically paired with a gray shade** that complements it."* ([themes/docs/theme/color](https://www.radix-ui.com/themes/docs/theme/color)). Scanning every `.mdx` under `data/themes/docs/` and `data/colors/docs/` for `second accent|secondary accent|two accents|multiple accents` yields **zero hits**. Emphasis is the **`variant`** axis, not a colour: *"Variants are visual variations of a component which are used to create visual hierarchies and communicate relative importance"* ([theme/overview](https://www.radix-ui.com/themes/docs/theme/overview)) — `classic`, `solid`, `soft`, `surface`, `outline`, `ghost`. Extra hues exist only for semantics and for *"multiplayer mode, where you assign a color to each user"* ([composing-a-palette](https://www.radix-ui.com/colors/docs/palette-composition/composing-a-palette)) — Radix's version of the meaningless-categorical firewall.

## 6. Does anyone argue a second accent is *needed*?

**No primary source found says a single accent is insufficient.** The affirmative case is M3's tertiary, whose stated job is *"balance"* and *"expression"* — aesthetic range, not a functional gap (§1).

The **counter**-case is better evidenced. NN/g: *"**Limit your palette to three colors.**"* … *"A small number of colors reinforces visual hierarchy and contrast because there is less for users to consider and get distracted by."* … *"**Reserve the accent color for what you want to stand out the most** on your page — for example, the primary call to action."* ([nngroup.com](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/color-enhance-design/)). And Adobe shipped a redesign that *subtracted* accent: *"To make experiences built in Spectrum 2 easier to parse we **removed a lot of extraneous highlight color** in common controls to reserve it for the most high-attention, high-action moments."* ([adobe.design](https://adobe.design/stories/design-for-scale/introducing-spectrum-2)).

**Refactoring UI is routinely misquoted here.** Its actual line is *"Most sites need one, **maybe** two colors that are used for primary actions"*, and its famous "you need more colors than you think" is about *shades per colour* (*"as many as ten different colors with 5-10 shades each"*) and about **semantic** colours — *"every site needs a few accent colors for **communicating different things to the user**"* ([refactoringui.com](https://refactoringui.com/previews/building-your-color-palette)). That argues for status hues, not a second brand accent. Nathan Curtis concurs: *"Every organization has one, two, or no more than a few core brand colors … The more choices you provide, the tougher it'll be to control harmonic combinations."* ([EightShapes](https://medium.com/eightshapes-llc/color-in-design-systems-a1c80f65fa3)).

⚠️ **[FOLKLORE] the 60-30-10 rule.** NN/g repeats it (*"Use 60% for the dominant color, 30% for the secondary color, and 10% towards an accent color"*, same URL) but cites no research, and **no design system endorses it in its docs**; its provenance is interior decorating. The palette-limiting claim it smuggles in is independently supported; the ratios are numerology. The large volume of "3-colour rule" SEO listicles traces to nothing and was excluded.

`[UNVERIFIED]`: no verbatim "use accent sparingly" sentence exists on primer.style (nearest is the *primary button* "use sparingly"). Tufte's *"Above all, do no harm"* could not be confirmed against the source. Spectrum quotes were recovered from the site's RSC payload (docs are JS-rendered) — verbatim CMS text, not visually diffed. Polaris's live colour pages 301 to a stub, so quotes come from the `Shopify/polaris` repo `main` that the site builds from. Atlassian's per-appearance button *prose* is client-rendered and unreachable; its appearance list and colours come from the shipped npm package instead.

## 7. Answers

**Q1 — genuine convention, or Bootstrap artifact? Largely a Bootstrap artifact, and a misread one.** The *word pair* is ubiquitous; the *concept* "second brand hue" is not. Bootstrap popularised `.btn-primary`/`.btn-secondary`, where secondary is `$gray-600` and officially means "lighter text / disabled / dividers" — it replaced `.text-muted`. Downstream libraries copied the **names** and kept the **greys** (shadcn chroma 0, Preline stone-900, Spectrum gray-300, Carbon Gray 80, Polaris gray[6]). Systems that never passed through Bootstrap independently converged on *one* accent and named the second rung something else entirely — `default` (Primer, Atlassian), a `variant` (Radix), a prominence slot (Polaris). **Real as vocabulary, false as colour theory.**

**Q2 — when a system has a "secondary", what is it?**

| System | Secondary is | Category |
|---|---|---|
| M3 `TONAL_SPOT` (+ CONTENT/FIDELITY/RAINBOW/NEUTRAL/FRUIT_SALAD) | `(h, 16)` vs primary `(h, 36)` | **(a)** same hue, lower chroma |
| M3 `EXPRESSIVE` / `VIBRANT` | piecewise-rotated hue, chroma 24 | **(b)** different hue — *derived, not authored* |
| Bootstrap | `$gray-600 #6c757d` | **(c)** neutral |
| shadcn/ui | `oklch(0.97 0 0)` | **(c)** neutral (chroma 0) |
| Preline | `stone-900` | **(c)** neutral |
| Carbon | `#393939` Gray 80 (button); `#0043ce` Blue 70 (link) | **(c)** neutral / (a) darker step |
| Spectrum | `gray-300` (button variant) | **(c)** neutral |
| Polaris | `gray[6]`; a *prominence* grammar slot | **(d)** position, resolving neutral |
| Primer / Atlassian / Radix | **does not exist** (`default`, `default`, `variant`) | **(d)** absent |
| daisyUI | `oklch(65% 0.241 354)` magenta | **(b)** genuine second hue — **optional** |

**Q3 — what breaks with one accent? Nothing functional.** No published source argues otherwise. Two systems argue the opposite in shipped form: Polaris forbids *"multiple brand roles in the same area"*, and Spectrum 2 removed accent to reserve it. NN/g's "limit to three colours / reserve the accent for what you want to stand out most" is the closest general statement, and it **agrees with** `style-guide.md`'s *"One accent: pick one color for `accent`. Two accents erases the focal signal."* So do Radix's single-`accentColor` model and Spectrum's chromostereopsis warning. **Nothing found contradicts the repo's existing position.** The one real gap a single accent leaves is **semantic** colour (error/success/warning) — a different axis, already handled here as a separate channel (`CURATION.md` Stage 5; M3 likewise hard-codes error at hue 25/chroma 84 rather than deriving it).

**Q4 — optional to *choose*, not optional to *exist*.** The distinction resolves the seed question: **you can decline to choose a secondary; you cannot ship without one.** M3 auto-derives it (no way to supply it via `fromSeed`), yet every scheme always *contains* a secondary palette — decline and your nav indicators, selected chips and tonal buttons render in M3's baseline purple. daisyUI's docs say "optional". shadcn/Preline ship neutral defaults nobody re-brands. Primer, Atlassian, Radix, Carbon and Polaris have no such token at all. **Authored: optional everywhere. Present: mandatory wherever the role exists.** That argues for *imputing* it, not *requiring* it in the seed.

## 8. Bearing on this repo

1. **`accent.secondary` should not be `Required: yes` in the seed table.** No surveyed system requires an authored second accent. Making it mandatory forces brands to invent a colour they do not own — the exact failure already recorded in `themecheck.py`'s docstring, where an impossible token caused a silent substitution of "a cyan that V2 does not own".
2. **The four candidate jobs in `CURATION.md` are not equally supported.** "Anchor a second walk" has no precedent. "Occupy slot 1 of the single walk" is categorical data-viz, which every system firewalls from UI accent (Spectrum, Atlassian, Radix). "Supply the diverging pole" is contradicted by ADR-013, which gates poles on CVD separation, not brand. The fourth — "the non-categorical UI accent (links, selection)" — is the only one with cross-system backing, and the evidence sharpens it: **the job is `selected/active state`, and it belongs to a *container* token, not a second hue.** Confirmed in three independent M3 implementations and derivable at chroma 16 from the primary.
3. **The existing split is already the better one.** richdocs ADR-012 divides `accent` by *contrast duty* (`accent` fill / `onAccent` / `link` text-safe), and `color_theming.md` varies "primary vs secondary vs background" *within one hue family* by saturation/lightness — structurally the same move M3 makes, and the same shape as M3's container/on-container pairs: a role defined by *what surface it paints*, not by which brand colour it is. That is the load-bearing distinction; a second brand hue is not.

**Answering "[OPEN] one walk or two?": one.** It depended on the secondary-accent question, and the evidence does not support a second anchor.
